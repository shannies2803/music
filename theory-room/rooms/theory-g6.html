The Grade 6 exam

Format
On paper, at an exam centre
Length
3 hours, 5 questions
Marks
Out of 100
Pass
66
Merit
80
Distinction
90
  • 1 Figured bass: chords in four parts or keyboard style above a given bass (15) · 2 Chords for a melody, or a bass with figures (15) · 3 Composing a melody for an instrument, from a choice (20) · 4 Questions on a score (25) · 5 Questions on an orchestral score (25).
  • Grade 6 is taken on set session dates, so check the booking deadline early.
  • No notes or books are allowed. Bring pencils, a rubber and a ruler.
  • A Grade 5 theory pass already covers what ABRSM practical Grades 6–8 ask for, so Grade 6 theory is for their own musicianship: harmony, composing and reading scores.

Printables: everything you need

  • Lesson packs: every day in the plan has a printable pack (button on the day's card) with what to say, a worked example, picture cards, questions to do together with answers, and their worksheet with an answer key.
  • Our practice papers, with a marking key and a place to record their marks:
  • Optional extras: ABRSM's own Grade 6 past papers (with model answers) are the best extra practice, especially for questions 1–3, the writing questions.
  • Wide-stave manuscript paper. The exam is written by hand, so your child should write on paper often, not only on screen.
  • Free lessons on every ABRSM topic at My Music Theory.
  • Their own pieces: find cadences, inversions and passing notes in real pieces.

Keeping them going

  • Stop while your child is still enjoying it. Short and regular beats long and tired.
  • Praise explanations more than speed ("you knew why").
  • When your child guesses, ask "what rule would help us decide?" before giving the answer.
  • Fast-track: three quick correct answers on three different days makes a topic green. Stop drilling it after that.
  • A mistake leaves their list after your child gets it right twice, so the list stays short.
