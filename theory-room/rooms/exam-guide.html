ABRSM or Trinity?

Both boards run graded exams from Grade 1 to Grade 8, recognised around the world, and both offer theory exams. The main difference in the practical exam is choice: ABRSM sets the same four parts for everyone, while Trinity lets you choose some of what you're tested on.

ABRSM also offers Performance Grades, which are filmed: four pieces and no scales, sight-reading or aural. For ABRSM Grades 6–8 practical exams, you need a pass in ABRSM Grade 5 Music Theory (or one of the alternatives the board lists) first.

What happens in the room

  1. The examiner says hello and may ask your name and which pieces you're playing. Give them your music list if your board asks for one.
  2. You can usually choose the order. Many people start with a piece to settle their nerves, then do scales while their fingers are warm. Ask if you'd like a different order.
  3. Scales: the examiner asks for a few from the list for your grade. Play each from memory, at a steady speed, with the right articulation.
  4. Sight-reading: you get about half a minute to look (and try out bits, on ABRSM). Use it: key, time signature, the highest and lowest notes, and any tricky rhythm.
  5. Aural (ABRSM, or Trinity if you choose it): the examiner plays and you sing, clap or answer questions. Answer in full sentences when asked about the music.
  6. The examiner may stop you before the end of a piece. That's normal: they've heard enough to give the mark.

What examiners listen for

Pieces

  • Pitch and rhythm: the right notes, with a steady pulse.
  • Tone: a good sound, in tune.
  • Shape: dynamics, phrasing and articulation as written.
  • Character: the mood and style of the piece, communicated with confidence.

Scales and arpeggios

  • Right notes, from memory.
  • Even rhythm and a sensible, steady speed.
  • Even tone across the range, and the articulation asked for.
  • Prompt, confident starts. Hesitation costs marks.

Sight-reading

  • Keep going. A steady pulse matters more than every note.
  • The right key and rhythm, then dynamics and articulation.
  • Choose a speed you can keep for the whole piece.

Aural

  • Answer promptly and clearly. Listen to the whole extract before deciding.
  • Use musical words: staccato, crescendo, minor key, perfect cadence.
  • When singing, pitch matters more than voice quality.

Where theory marks usually go

These are the mistakes that come up again and again. Read the list before every mock.

Reading the question

  • Answering in the wrong clef, or not noticing the clef changes.
  • Missing "without key signature" (or "with key signature").
  • Naming an interval from the top note instead of the bottom.
  • Writing a scale ascending when it asked for descending, or one octave when it asked for two.

Rhythm

  • Bars that don't add up. Count every bar of your answers.
  • Rests in the wrong place: show the beats clearly.
  • Forgetting that a dotted crotchet is the beat in 6/8.
  • Beaming across the middle of a 4/4 bar.

Keys and intervals

  • Key signatures in the wrong order or on the wrong line.
  • Forgetting the raised 7th in a minor key (and the raised 6th going up in the melodic minor).
  • Mixing up augmented and diminished.
  • Missing an accidental earlier in the bar.

Harmony (Grades 6–8)

  • Consecutive 5ths and octaves.
  • Doubling the leading note, or the 3rd of a major chord without good reason.
  • Parts too far apart, crossing, or out of range.
  • A cadence that doesn't fit the phrase, or a melody that doesn't fit the harmony.

The last four weeks

Four weeks to go

  • Theory: take a timed mock. Revise the weakest topics it shows.
  • Practical: play your pieces through to someone, as if it were the exam.
  • Run through every scale on the list in Examiner mode.

Three and two weeks

  • A written paper each week, marked with the answer key.
  • Sight-reading every day: one new short piece.
  • One aural mock a week, and the tests you find hardest.

The last week

  • Nothing new. Keep everything ticking over.
  • One last mock early in the week, then light review.
  • Sleep well, and check the time and place.

The Theory tab can make you a week-by-week plan for any board and grade: pick the exam date under Revision.

On the day

Practical exam checklist

  • Your music, and the board's rules on copies
  • Your instrument, spare strings or reeds, rosin, mutes
  • Your list of pieces, if your board asks for one
  • Arrive 10–15 minutes early to warm up and tune
  • Your accompanist knows the time and place
  • A drink of water (not in the exam room)

Theory exam checklist

  • Pencils, a good eraser and a sharpener
  • Your entry details or login
  • For online exams: a charged device, a quiet room, and the board's practice test done beforehand
  • A watch to plan your time
  • Leave 10 minutes at the end to check every answer

Nerves

  • Breathe out slowly before you start.
  • Take your time between pieces. Nobody is rushing you.
  • If something goes wrong, keep going. Examiners mark the whole performance, not one slip.
  • Examiners want you to do well.
