ABRSM & Trinity Repertoire

Current graded exam lists from both boards, Initial to Grade 8, for strings, harp, woodwind, brass, percussion, guitar, piano, organ, keyboard and singing, plus the ARSM, LRSM and FRSM and ATCL, LTCL and FTCL performance diplomas for every instrument and voice that has them. Each piece has a note, its length, period, difficulty within the grade, playing style and a rating, with links to scores and recordings. Filter or regroup the lists, see which pieces both boards set, and star pieces to build a programme that is checked against the exam’s rules.

No theory requirement. Digital options: Technical Work pathway, or Repertoire-only (4 pieces at 25 marks each; singing Grades 6–8: 5 songs at 20).
Level 4 ARSM30-minute performance (±2 min), at least 20 min from the ARSM list plus up to 10 min own choice. No written part. In person or video. Marked out of 50: pass 34, merit 40, distinction 45. Needs Grade 8 (ABRSM Practical or Performance Grade, or an accepted equivalent such as Trinity) on the same or a closely related instrument. ATCL32–38 min recital plus a written programme (4 of the 100 marks are for the programme and its planning; no programme notes). Marked out of 100: pass 60, distinction 80 (no merit). In person or video. No prerequisite.
Level 6 LRSM45-minute (±10%) filmed recital (half or more from the list) plus a 2,000-word written piece or a 10-minute spoken video. Marked out of 75: pass 50, merit 60, distinction 68. Needs Grade 8; ARSM not required first. LTCL37–43 min recital, out of 100: pass 60, distinction 80. Written programme as for ATCL; no programme notes. In person or video. No prerequisite; ATCL not needed first.
Level 7 FRSM55-minute (±10%) filmed recital plus 3,500 words or a 17-minute spoken video. Same marking as LRSM. LRSM not required first. FTCL42–48 min recital, in person or video, with written programme notes on every piece. Result is Approved or Not Approved (no marks). No formal prerequisite, but Trinity strongly advises LTCL or equivalent first.

Difficulty is relative to the level the piece is set at: 1 is at the easier end of that list, 3 is typical, 5 is at the top end. It compares pieces within one list only, not across levels.

Rating is our judgement of the piece as an exam choice: how good the music is, how well it shows a player off, and how well the result rewards the preparation time. 5★ pieces are standouts; 3★ is a solid choice. It is a guide for shortlisting, not a board ranking.

Length is a typical performance time for exactly what is set (that movement or those movements). Players vary by a minute or so either way.

Pieces tagged LESS-KNOWN are new or rarely recorded; our notes on these are provisional, so listen to a recording or look at the score before choosing.
